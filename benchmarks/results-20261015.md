# Paired pixel-core pilot — 2026-10-15

The pilot supports complementary-view geometry recovery in clean fixtures, but
it does **not** establish that fusion is generally more usable or accurate than
either camera. Shared calibration error remains an accepted-wrong failure mode.
No thresholds, gates or estimator source were tuned after observing these results.

## Population and interpretation

600 cached scenes in20 cells: 30 seeds ×10 conditions ×2 modes. These produce
14,400 sensor requests across 24 method settings and600 prior references.
The independent unit is a scene within a condition, never a frame or method row.
Seeds100000–100029 are pilot-only. Conditions share poses where appropriate;
do not pool them as independent samples. All masks are oracle test masks.

The following tables use the shipped declared-noise precision and Huber loss.
Each usable count has denominator30 and requires all primary spatial limits,
including both required dimensions in geometry mode. Missing dimensions are
incomplete; accepted estimates can therefore be neither wrong nor usable.
The full summary retains all four precision/loss settings, exact confidence
limits, rejections, accepted-only error quantiles and scene-paired bootstrap intervals.

## Consequential observations

- Clean geometry: A+B is complete and usable30/30; neither single-camera subset
  gives a complete usable result. Weak complementary geometry has the same
 30/30 versus0/30 result. The paired usability difference is1.0 with bootstrap
  interval[1.0,1.0] in this finite pilot. That degenerate pilot interval is not
  a population guarantee.
- Noisy pose at equal time: A29/30, B27/30, A+B29/30 are usable. A+B minus A has
  paired interval[-0.10,0.10]. A+B position p95 is0.737mm versus A0.676mm.
  This does not show an advantage over the better single camera.
- Noisy geometry: A+B has0/30 usable at equal time and0/30 at equal samples.
  The one equal-sample accepted estimate lacks required complete geometry.
  Rejection cannot be omitted to claim conditional accuracy as overall usability.
- Shared calibration bias, pose: A+B accepts29/30 at equal time, all29 spatially
  wrong, with10.452mm accepted position p95. Equal samples accepts30/30, all wrong.
  Agreement between cameras cannot validate a shared calibration error.
- The95% full joint ellipsoid covers29/29 accepted shared-bias pose states.
  Thus this case fails task accuracy despite coverage. High coverage in a small
  accepted pilot does not establish calibrated covariance or real-sensor robustness.
  Camera B alone under its individual calibration fault covers0/28 accepted
  states. Fusion rejects all30 of those conflicting-view requests.

Zero wrong among30 accepted has a one-sided95% upper bound of about9.50%,
so even the clean cells cannot establish the1% target. Zero wrong among200
accepted would still give about1.49%. No false-acceptance qualification is claimed.

## Scope limits and residual controls

Equal raw surface budgets give potential surface caps100 for each subset in
static non-dropout fixtures. Moving `tf_gap` scenes retain small cap imbalances;
see the independent audit for per-cell ranges. Dropout retains shortages rather
than replenishing absent-camera data. Boundary group caps vary with observed
axes/lines. Equal raw counts are not equal information.

`tf_gap` uses explicit correct matrices in this stage: it does not exercise TF
admission. Transport, timestamp mismatch, ROS goal latency and estimate age are
not tested by these rows. End-to-end usability is null. Method execution order
is fixed and the host is shared, so small timing differences are not causal evidence.

The scene family uses a narrow pose range, fixed prior offset, and width/length
increases of12–25mm. Size decreases, wider poses, segmentation errors, real depth
correlations and actual grasp clearance are not established. `correct_prior`
means correct pose; geometry mode still starts with wrong nominal dimensions.
